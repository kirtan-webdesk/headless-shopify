# 10 — SEO Baseline (Headless)

> **A headless rebuild changes routing whether or not anyone intended it to.**
>
> Every other failure in this arm is discovered by us. This one is discovered by the client, in analytics, in the week after launch — which is why it is the failure that damages the relationship most.

---

## 1. What the theme was doing that nobody scoped

In a theme, the platform generated URLs, canonicals, a sitemap, robots directives, product structured data and social tags. None of it appeared in a scope of work because none of it was work.

**Headless keeps none of it.** The routes are yours, so everything derived from routes is yours.

| In a theme | In headless |
|---|---|
| URL structure generated by the platform | **You define every route.** A path that differs by one segment is a lost page. |
| Canonical tags emitted automatically | Emitted by your code, per route, or not at all |
| `sitemap.xml` maintained by the platform | Yours to generate and keep current |
| `robots.txt` platform-managed | Yours |
| Product / breadcrumb structured data from the theme | Yours to emit, per template |
| Title, description, Open Graph, Twitter cards | Yours, per route |
| Pagination and faceted-URL handling | Yours, including the crawl-budget consequences |

This table is the estimating argument. It is also review point 7 of the discovery audit — the effort-impact statement naming the delta versus a theme build.

---

## 2. The URL inventory and redirect map

**The single highest-value artifact in this file, and it is launch-blocking.**

Produced during discovery (`12-discovery-audit.md`), not during build. Contents:

1. **Every live URL** — from the platform, from analytics (anything with sessions in the last 12 months), from Search Console (anything with impressions), and from the existing sitemap. Four sources, because each one misses something the others catch.
2. **Every inbound-linked URL**, including ones no longer linked internally. An old campaign landing page with three good backlinks is worth more than a category page nobody visits.
3. **The new path** for each, or an explicit decision that it has none.
4. **The redirect rule**, one per old URL. `301` unless there is a stated reason otherwise.
5. **The deliberate drops** — URLs intentionally not carried, recorded alongside the dropped-functionality column the client signs.

**Rules:**

- **Redirect to the equivalent page, not to the homepage.** A mass redirect to `/` is indistinguishable from a soft 404 and throws away the link equity the redirect existed to preserve.
- **No redirect chains.** Old → new, one hop. Chains accumulate across successive replatforms and each hop costs.
- **Test the map before launch, not after.** Every entry, automated, against the staging host.
- **Trailing slash, case, and query-parameter handling are decisions**, made once and applied consistently. Inconsistency here generates duplicate URLs at scale.

**A launch without a tested redirect map is a launch with unmeasured traffic risk.** That is a `D-QA-GATE-BLOCK`-class condition, not a post-launch task.

## 3. Canonicals, pagination and facets

- **Every indexable route emits a self-referencing canonical** unless there is a stated reason it points elsewhere.
- **Faceted and filtered URLs** — decide per engagement which are indexable. Filter combinations multiply, and an unbounded indexable facet space burns crawl budget on pages nobody searches for.
- **Pagination** — decide the pattern and hold it. Page 2+ generally canonicalises to itself, not to page 1; canonicalising a paginated series to its first page tells crawlers the deeper items do not exist.
- **Parameter handling** — session IDs, tracking parameters and sort orders must not generate distinct indexable URLs.
- **One canonical host and protocol.** Decide `www` vs apex and enforce it at the edge, once.

---

## 4. Structured data

Emitted by your code now, per template. Nothing generates it for you.

- Product pages: product data including price and availability, and **it must match what the page actually shows.** Structured data that disagrees with the rendered price is worse than none — it is a trust penalty and, if the price is wrong, a customer-facing one.
- Breadcrumbs matching the actual navigation path.
- Organisation and site-level markup, once, in the right place.
- **Structured data is generated from the same data the page renders.** If it comes from a second query or a hardcoded block, the two drift and nobody notices until they disagree.
- Validate against a structured-data testing tool as a build step, not as a launch-week discovery.

---

## 5. Metadata and social

Per route, from real data — never a template default that shipped with the starter.

- Title and meta description, per route, with a defined fallback chain that never resolves to the site name alone.
- Open Graph and Twitter card tags, including a resolvable absolute image URL. Relative image paths in OG tags are the most common social-preview bug.
- Language and direction attributes set correctly on the document.
- **Metadata must be present in the server-rendered HTML.** Metadata injected client-side after hydration is metadata some crawlers and every social scraper will not see.

---

## 6. Sitemap and robots

- **Generate the sitemap from the same source of truth the routes come from.** A hand-maintained sitemap is wrong within a sprint.
- Include only indexable, canonical, `200`-returning URLs. A sitemap full of redirects and 404s is a quality signal in the wrong direction.
- Segment large sitemaps by type and use an index.
- Keep it current — a nightly or on-publish regeneration, not a build-time snapshot that goes stale between deploys.
- **`robots.txt` is yours now.** Staging and preview environments must be non-indexable, and that must be verified rather than assumed (see §9).
- The `noindex` on staging must **not** ship to production. This is a real and recurring launch failure, and it is silent — the site simply disappears from search over the following days.

---

## 7. Internationalisation

Only where the engagement has multiple markets or languages.

- `hreflang` is reciprocal and self-referencing. A one-directional set is ignored.
- Market and locale belong in the **cache key** (`HL-CACHE-001`). A cached page served to the wrong market is both an SEO and a commercial error.
- Currency and market selection must not be inferred silently from IP in a way that changes what a crawler sees versus what a user sees.
- **Shopify Markets per-plan capability is an open `TODO-VERIFY`** (register open item 3). **Nothing about multi-market or multi-currency headless may be written, estimated or quoted until it lands.** Name it as an open question; do not price it.

---

## 8. Rendering strategy and crawlers

Restating `06-data-layer-patterns.md` and `04-performance-budget.md` from the crawler's side:

- **Content that must rank must be in the server-rendered HTML.** Client-fetched content is a bet on the crawler's rendering budget, and it is a bet you do not need to take.
- A route's rendering strategy is declared with its cache behaviour before build (`HL-CACHE-002`).
- Revalidation windows need a purge path (`HL-ISR-001`) and a stated staleness tolerance (`HL-ISR-002`). **A stale price in structured data is a customer-facing error, not just an SEO one.**
- Soft 404s — a "not found" page returning `200` — are a specific headless failure, because in a theme the platform returned the status for you. **A missing product returns 404. A removed product with a successor returns 301.**

---

## 9. Verification

**Before launch:**

| Check | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| Redirect map tested | Every entry, automated, against staging. Zero chains, zero homepage-fallbacks that should have been equivalents. |
| Status codes | 404 for missing, 301 for moved, 200 only for real pages |
| Canonicals | Present and correct per template |
| Metadata in server HTML | Verified with JS disabled, not in devtools after hydration |
| Structured data | Validates, and matches rendered content |
| Sitemap | Generated, current, canonical URLs only |
| **`robots.txt` and `noindex`** | Staging blocked; **production explicitly not blocked** |
| Search Console | Property ready for the new property/protocol before launch, not after |

**After launch** — because launch is when this file's failure mode actually appears:

- Watch index coverage and 404s daily for the first two weeks.
- Watch top-landing-page traffic against the pre-launch baseline. **Capture that baseline before launch** — you cannot measure a drop against a number you never recorded.
- Keep the redirect map live and add to it. A missed URL surfacing in week two is normal and fixable; noticing it in month three is not.

---

## Anti-patterns

1. Treating SEO as a post-launch task when the redirect map is a discovery deliverable.
2. Building the URL inventory from the sitemap alone, and missing everything with backlinks but no internal links.
3. Redirecting old URLs to the homepage instead of the equivalent page.
4. Shipping redirect chains that accumulate across successive replatforms.
5. Launching with an untested redirect map.
6. Failing to capture the pre-launch traffic baseline, then arguing about whether traffic dropped.
7. Returning `200` for a not-found page, because the platform used to set the status.
8. Injecting metadata client-side and assuming crawlers and social scrapers will run the JS.
9. Relative image URLs in Open Graph tags.
10. Shipping the starter template's default title and description, unchanged, on some routes.
11. Generating structured data from a different source than the rendered page, and letting the two drift.
12. Emitting a price in structured data that does not match the rendered price.
13. Letting staging's `noindex` reach production — the silent failure where the site simply disappears from search.
14. Hand-maintaining a sitemap, or generating one at build time and letting it go stale between deploys.
15. Listing redirects and 404s in the sitemap.
16. Canonicalising a paginated series to page 1, telling crawlers the deeper items do not exist.
17. Leaving the facet space unbounded and indexable, and burning crawl budget on filter combinations.
18. Writing or quoting anything about Shopify Markets multi-market capability while its per-plan behaviour is an open `TODO-VERIFY`.
19. Omitting market or locale from a cache key.
20. Assuming SEO parity is free because "the content is the same content."
